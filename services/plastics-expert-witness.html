Plastics & Polymer Expert Witness

Independent polymer-science opinion on why a plastic part failed, and whether the material, the design or the process was responsible.

Request a Conflict Check Expert Witness Overview

Disputes this covers

  • Product liability — a plastic component cracked, fractured, leaked or degraded in service, and cause is contested.
  • Supply and specification disputes — whether the resin, compound or moulded part delivered was the one specified, and whether a substituted grade was equivalent.
  • Manufacturing disputes — whether a moulding, extrusion or compounding process was run in a way that damaged the material.
  • Insurance and subrogation — a loss that traces back to a plastic fitting, housing, pipe or container.

The technical questions that usually decide the matter

  • Was the fracture brittle or ductile, and where did it start?
  • Is there evidence of environmental stress cracking, and what chemical was the part exposed to?
  • Has the polymer degraded — by hydrolysis, oxidation, heat or ultraviolet light — and did that happen in processing or in service?
  • Does the material in the failed part match the specified grade, including its additives and any regrind content?
  • Did moulded-in stress, weld lines, voids or contamination put a defect where the load was highest?
  • Was the material a reasonable choice for the temperature, load, chemicals and service life it was actually given?

Why this sits inside the principal’s field

Polymer science and engineering is the core discipline of this practice: it is the subject of the principal’s doctorate, of his professorship at The University of Southern Mississippi, and of the larger part of his refereed publications. The material classes covered include engineering plastics, polyolefins, polyamides, polyesters and polycarbonates, styrenics, polyurethanes and thermosets.
